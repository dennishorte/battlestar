# Rise of Ix

## Rules

Rise of Ix adds an Ix board, a CHOAM overlay with a Shipping track, Tech tiles, dreadnoughts, a Freighter for each player, and new cards. These notes are for playing it with Dune: Imperium — Uprising. Where the original expansion names a board space Uprising does not have, the Uprising board is used instead.

### Setup with Uprising

1. Fold the CHOAM overlay in half and cover only the top-right corner of the Uprising board. This adds the CHOAM section (Smuggling, Interstellar Shipping, and the Shipping track) and covers Assembly Hall and Gather Support. The other Landsraad spaces stay.
2. Place the Ix board beside the game board. It holds Tech Negotiation, Dreadnought, and the three Tech-tile stacks.
3. Shuffle the 18 Tech tiles face down and split them into three stacks of six. Put a stack on each space of the Ix board and turn the top tile of each stack face up.
4. Each player puts two dreadnoughts in their supply and puts their Freighter disc on the bottom space of the Shipping track.
5. Shuffle the 17 Intrigue cards into the Intrigue deck and the 35 Imperium cards into the Imperium deck before dealing the Imperium Row. Add the six Leaders to the Leader pool.
6. The overlay and the Ix board have no observation posts printed on them. Play as if there are two:
   - One post connects to Interstellar Shipping and Smuggling.
   - One post connects to Tech Negotiation and Dreadnought.
7. Do not add the Rise of Ix Conflict cards to a normal game. Epic mode adds one of them, Economic Supremacy, and leaves the other three out. See Epic mode below.
8. Ilesa Ecaz needs the Foldspace cards from the original game. Only she may acquire them.
9. With the CHOAM module: after setting up the 20 standard contracts, shuffle the 10 Rise of Ix contracts and deal two to each player. Each player keeps one face up and the rest go back in the box.

On the original board, the full overlay covers Rally Troops, Hall of Oratory, Secure Contract, and Sell Melange. Uprising does not use that layout.

### Board spaces

Dreadnought and Tech Negotiation use the Landsraad (green) Agent icon. Smuggling and Interstellar Shipping use the Spice Trade (yellow) Agent icon. None of the four is a Combat space. Interstellar Shipping is the one that needs Influence.

| Space | Icon | Cost or requirement | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dreadnought | Landsraad | 3 Solari | Commission a dreadnought, Acquire Tech, or both |
| Tech Negotiation | Landsraad | none | Choose one: Acquire Tech at a 1 spice discount, or place a Negotiator on Ix. On your Reveal turn, +1 Persuasion if your Agent is still here |
| Smuggling | Spice Trade | none | Gain 1 Solari and move your Freighter once |
| Interstellar Shipping | Spice Trade | 2 or more Spacing Guild Influence | Move your Freighter twice |

"Commission a dreadnought and/or Acquire Tech" means each part is optional. You may do one, the other, or both, paying for whatever you choose.

### Shipping track

Each player has one Freighter, starting on the bottom space. A Freighter icon lets you do one of these:

- Advance one space up the track. You cannot advance if the Freighter is already on the top space.
- Recall the Freighter to the bottom space. You collect the reward for its current space and every space below that, in any order.

Advancing does not score. Only a recall pays. Recalling from the bottom space collects only the bottom reward.

| Space | Reward |
|---|---|
| Bottom | Choose one: Dividends, or 2 spice |
| Middle | Recruit 2 troops, and gain 1 Influence with any one Faction |
| Top | Acquire Tech with a discount of 2 spice |

Dividends: you gain 5 Solari, and each opponent gains 1 Solari.

Troops recruited from the track go to your garrison. If you sent an Agent to a Combat space this turn, you may deploy them under the usual deployment rules. Interstellar Shipping is not a Combat space, so troops recruited there go to the garrison.

### Tech tiles

Acquire Tech, on a board space or a card, is the only way to take a tile. Choose any face-up top tile, pay its spice cost, put the tile in your supply, gain its one-time acquire effect if it has one, then turn up the next tile in that stack. There is no limit to how many tiles you may own. An empty stack stays empty.

Two things can lower the spice cost. The cost never goes below 0.

- A Tech Discount of 1 or 2 spice printed on that Acquire Tech icon. Discounts from two different icons do not combine.
- Negotiators. The Tech Negotiation icon lets you place one troop from your supply on the Ix banner, left of the tiles. If you have no troop, nothing happens. When you later Acquire Tech, you may return any number of your Negotiators to your supply. Each one returned reduces that purchase by 1 spice. This does combine with a discount on the icon.

You cannot recruit a Negotiator off Ix except by returning it as part of a purchase. A troop you recruit must come from your supply.

Appropriate: when it lets you pay Solari instead of spice, you cannot mix the two currencies on one tile. Each Negotiator you return reduces that Solari cost by 1.

Tile abilities:

- An acquire effect happens once, when you buy the tile.
- An ability that names a time (Reveal turn, when you win a Conflict, round start, endgame) happens then.
- An ability with no stated time happens during one of your turns. Spy Satellites' "pay 3 spice and trash this for 1 Victory Point" is this kind. Its other line is Endgame only.
- A flip icon means once per round, on one of your Agent turns or your Reveal turn. Flip the tile face down when you use it. Turn it face up again at Round Start. The first English printing said Agent turns only; the later correction includes Reveal turns.

Chaumurky wins an endgame tie by itself. Spice, Solari, water, and garrisoned troops are not compared if one tied player has it.

### Dreadnoughts

Each player has two. The Dreadnought icon commissions one: take it from your supply and put it in your garrison. If you commissioned it while sending an Agent to a Combat space, you may deploy it to the Conflict. You cannot commission one if both are already out of your supply.

In a Conflict, each of your dreadnoughts is worth 3 strength. Prince Rhombur Vernius's dreadnoughts are worth 4. A dreadnought in the Conflict counts as having a unit there: your strength does not drop to 0 for lack of troops, your swords count, and you may play Combat Intrigue. A dreadnought still in your garrison does none of this.

Combat never sends a dreadnought back to your supply. It returns to your garrison.

- If you do not win the Conflict, every dreadnought you had in the Conflict goes back to your garrison.
- If you win with at least one dreadnought in the Conflict, one of them parks on a control space. Any other dreadnought you had in that Conflict goes back to your garrison immediately. The parked one also goes back to your garrison at the end of the next round's Combat, the round after it parked. It does not go back to your supply.

The space it parks on must be able to hold a Control marker and must not already have a dreadnought. On the Uprising board those spaces are Arrakeen, Spice Refinery, and Imperial Basin. (The original expansion names Arrakeen, Carthag, and Imperial Basin.) Place it on that space's flag, covering any Control marker already there.

While it sits there you receive that space's normal control bonus: 1 Solari for Arrakeen or Spice Refinery, 1 spice for Imperial Basin, and the defensive troop if a Conflict card awards one for a space you control. When it leaves, a covered Control marker is revealed again, and that player regains control.

"Unit" on a Rise of Ix card means a troop or a dreadnought. Cards from the original game that say "troop" do not affect dreadnoughts. Uprising already uses "unit" for troops and sandworms, and treats a dreadnought as a further unit type.

Deployment uses the same limit as troops: on a Combat space you may deploy any units you recruited this turn, plus up to two more units from your garrison.

### Icons

- **Discard.** Choose a card from your hand, not an Intrigue card, and put it in your discard pile. If the discard is a cost and you do not pay it, you do not get the effect.
- **Unload.** A Reveal box with this icon also triggers when the card is discarded or trashed. On your Reveal turn it works as a normal Reveal box and the icon is ignored. The card does not discard or trash itself unless it says so. Treachery is an exception: its Agent box trashes itself, which triggers the Unload Reveal box.
- **Infiltration.** The Agent icon with a partial Agent profile lets you send an Agent to a space of that icon even if an enemy Agent is there, including more than one. You may still use the icon on an empty space. You cannot infiltrate a space where you already have an Agent. Infiltration does not ignore The Voice. Icons added by Dispatch an Envoy do not infiltrate, even if the card already had an Infiltration icon.
- **Epic Game Only.** The card is used only in Epic mode. Control the Spice is the one in this set.

### Conflict cards

A normal Uprising game does not use the Rise of Ix Conflict cards. Epic mode uses Economic Supremacy and none of the other three. Their rewards are recorded here so the cards are not a blank. The expansion has four: two Conflict I, one Conflict II, and one Conflict III. Their faces carry the two-dot Rise of Ix mark and no Crysknife, Desert Mouse, or Ornithopter icon. The two Skirmish faces are both titled Skirmish; the names below distinguish them.

| Card | Level | 1st | 2nd | 3rd |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skirmish I | I | Move your Freighter once and recruit 1 troop | 2 spice | 1 spice |
| Skirmish II | I | Move your Freighter once and 1 spice | 3 Solari | 2 Solari |
| Trade Monopoly | II | Move your Freighter twice and recruit 1 troop | 1 Intrigue card and 1 water | 1 Intrigue card or 1 water |
| Economic Supremacy | III | 1 Victory Point. You may pay 6 Solari for another Victory Point, and you may pay 4 spice for another | 1 Victory Point | 2 spice and 2 Solari |

A Conflict reward taken after your Reveal turn is not part of that Reveal turn. The official FAQ uses Trade Monopoly as the example: if winning it lets you recall from the top of the Shipping track and you buy Spaceport, the two cards you draw are kept for the next round, when you will also draw your normal five. That conflict is not in our Uprising decks.

### Leaders

Printed abilities that were only partly recorded before:

- **Archduke Armand Ecaz — Coordination.** On your Reveal turn, if you have at least two Agents on Landsraad, City, or Spice Trade spaces (the green, purple, and yellow icons; they need not match each other), you may trash one of your cards in play. **Conscript:** you may acquire a card that costs 3 Persuasion or less. The official FAQ says the two Agents are required.
- **Ilesa Ecaz — One Step Ahead.** At Round Start, set aside a card from your hand. At the start of your second turn this round, reveal it and return it to your hand. If you use it to send an Agent this turn: 1 spice if it has exactly one Agent icon, otherwise 1 Solari. "Otherwise" still requires that you sent the Agent with that card. If you do not use the card, you get nothing. If your first turn of the round is a Reveal turn, add the set-aside card to the revealed cards; you do not gain the spice or Solari. **Guild Contacts:** pay 1 Solari to gain a Foldspace card.
- **Tessia Vernius — Careful Observation.** Her four rewards are one track, taken from top to bottom, one at a time. A reward is not tied to an influence number or to a particular faction. Whenever you reach 2 Influence on a faction track, you take the next reward. Which faction that was does not matter.
  1. Discard a card to gain 1 spice. The discard is optional. If you do not discard, you do not gain the spice.
  2. Gain the bonus from this token's Influence track.
  3. Gain 1 Influence on this token's Influence track.
  4. Gain the bonus from this token's Influence track, and gain 1 Influence on that track.

  **Duplicity:** lose 1 Influence with any Faction, then gain 1 Influence with a Faction where you have a Snooper token.
- **Viscount Hundro Moritani — Intelligence.** At the start of the game, look at the top two cards of the Intrigue deck. Keep one and put the other back on top. In Epic mode he does this after every player has drawn their starting Intrigue card. **Couriers:** pay 1 spice to move your Freighter once. This is a Freighter icon, not a dreadnought.
- **Prince Rhombur Vernius — Heavy Lasgun Cannons.** Your dreadnoughts are worth 4 strength instead of 3. **Ixian Technology:** Acquire Tech, or use Tech Negotiation.
- **"Princess" Yuna Moritani — Smuggling Operation.** You start with no water. Each time you gain Solari on your turn, gain 1 more. This applies to every Solari gain that turn, including Dividends. Solari from Slig Farmer adds only 1 extra Solari from this ability. **Final Delivery:** pay 7 Solari to gain 1 Influence of your choice, 1 troop, and 1 spice.

### Epic mode

Optional. Play to 12 Victory Points. During setup:

- Build the Conflict deck with no Conflict I cards: five random Uprising Conflict II cards on top of five Conflict III cards. The five Conflict III cards are Uprising's four, plus Economic Supremacy. Leave Skirmish I, Skirmish II, and Trade Monopoly out.
- Each player replaces one Dune, the Desert Planet in their starting deck with Control the Spice.
- Each player draws an Intrigue card. Hundro waits until the others have drawn, then uses Intelligence.
- Each player starts with 5 troops in their garrison instead of 3.

### Solo and two-player rivals

A rival is not a player in the six-player game. In the physical game, rivals are the automated House Hagal opponents used in solo play and in the two-player game. The six-player variant is a separate team mode. This game has no solo mode and no rivals. The notes below are only a record of those cards. The nine House Hagal card faces (sword counts and the exact alternate space on each split card) and the two Rival reference cards were not found as a full transcription.

Setup of the House Hagal deck:

- Remove both Hall of Oratory cards and both Rally Troops cards from the original deck.
- Shuffle in the nine new cards, then remove some of them. Solo: remove the two Dreadnought cards marked 2P. Two players: remove the four cards marked 1P, which are two Dreadnought cards and two Tech Negotiation cards.
- With Uprising, also remove the Uprising House Hagal cards for Accept Contract, Shipping (two), Gather Support (two), and Assembly Hall. Drop the Rise of Ix Foldspace / Interstellar Shipping card. For a card that would send the rival to Foldspace, send it to Deliver Supplies instead.

Rival behavior from the rulebook:

- A Dreadnought card for a rival that already has two dreadnoughts is skipped. Reveal another card.
- Interstellar Shipping still requires 2 Spacing Guild Influence. Each such card gives an alternate for a rival who is short: skip it, or send the Agent to a different space printed on the card.
- A rival's Freighter always advances until it reaches the middle space, then recalls. It always chooses Dividends, not spice. In a solo game it gains 5 Solari from its own Dividends, and every other player gains 1. A solo rival also gains 1 Solari whenever anyone else chooses Dividends. Influence from the middle space goes to the Faction where it has the least Influence. On a tie, the player with the First Player marker chooses, or the player who acts next if the rival has that marker.
- A rival deploys dreadnoughts before troops, whenever it can. When it must park a dreadnought, it covers an opponent's Control marker if it can, and does not cover its own. If several spaces remain, it prefers Imperial Basin, then Arrakeen, then Carthag on the original board. On the Uprising board, use the control spaces that exist.
- Solo rivals can buy only Tech tiles marked with the small House Hagal icon. If no face-up tile has that mark, trash the top tile of the largest stack (the topmost stack if several are tied) and reveal the next. The rival buys the most expensive marked tile it can afford, breaking ties by the topmost stack. It will not spend 7 spice for a Victory Point unless the current Conflict is a Conflict III. In a two-player game the rival ignores Tech tiles entirely.
- At Tech Negotiation, a solo rival Acquires Tech at a 1 spice discount if it can. If it cannot afford any tile and has a troop, it places a Negotiator instead, and uses its Negotiators on the next purchase.
- Rival instructions printed for specific tiles: Detonation Devices always takes the Victory Point. Flagship, on a Combat space with 4 Solari and at least three troops in supply, pays, flips, and recruits and deploys three troops. Holtzman Engine is worth 1 Victory Point at endgame; the rival is treated as having two The Spice Must Flow. Invasion Ships flips to avoid a block and does not pay the discard cost. Sonic Snoopers draws the Intrigue card when acquired and is never trashed. Spy Satellites is trashed for a Victory Point if the rival has 3 spice on its turn. Training Drones flips at the first chance each round, including the moment it is acquired. Troop Transports deploys every troop it recruits from the Shipping track, unless Expert Troop Deployment is in use.
- Prince Rhombur Vernius and Viscount Hundro Moritani can be solo rivals. Rhombur's Signet Ring Acquires Tech if he can, otherwise Tech Negotiation. Hundro's Signet Ring spends 1 spice for the Freighter icon if he has the spice.
- A solo rival collects troop rewards from the new Conflict cards.
- Optional solo variants: after each of your Reveal turns, roll two dice and trash the Imperium Row cards in those positions, counting from the left, ignoring a 6 and trashing only one card if the dice match. Brutal Escalation: in a Conflict III, add the swords from two freshly revealed House Hagal cards to the rival's strength.

The rulebook does not list every tile that bears the House Hagal icon. The eight tiles above are the ones it gives rival instructions for.

### Card clarifications from the official FAQ and rulebook

- Court Intrigue and Sonic Snoopers: a card placed on the bottom of the Intrigue deck is not shown to opponents.
- Imperial Bashar: the Reveal box counts only other cards that actually provide swords this turn.
- Second Wave: you must already have a unit in the Conflict before you can play it, as with any Combat Intrigue.
- Guild Chief Administrator: discarding a card from your hand can trash a card from hand, discard, or play. An Unload card discarded or trashed this way triggers its Reveal box.
